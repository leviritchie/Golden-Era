# Documentation Guide

Status: active navigation and evidence policy. This file does not prove runtime behavior.

## Truth Order

When prose disagrees with implementation, use this order:

1. Current source, profiles, manifests, and packed-data generators.
2. Contract validators run against the same worktree.
3. Generated artifacts produced by that worktree.
4. Installed payload readback from the active Golden Era install.
5. Logs, live-bridge observations, and decomp evidence tied to a named build.
6. Focused runtime or user-visible gameplay tests.

A higher proof tier does not imply the next one. In particular, a generated map or successful build does not prove installation, startup, interaction dispatch, pathfinding, video playback, or campaign carry behavior.

- `homm3_reference_frame_skeleton_binding.md`: owned bind for AI-generated meshes solved from an *exact* animation reference (registered capture + DEF plates), with the acceptance measured against the frame's own DEF coverage instead of an image score. Weights are a function of vertex position (patch identity and marker bones are not used), and the audit reports tearing, surface cohesion and escape regions beside containment, so neither a hole nor a prop fused to the wrong limb can ship as a lower-stretch improvement. Escape regions name, per group of vertices that leave their own frame's plate, the bone driving them and the reference joint the plate has there; field sharpness (`--power`) is the knob and its head-versus-tearing trade is tabulated rather than settled. A prop region that cannot be bound by any weighting is decided by measurement rather than by taste: `owned_rerig/prop_fit.py` (run by `tools/homm3_meshy_frame_pose/prop_region_fit.py`) tries every flesh bone's rigid transform against every frame's own plate and reports whether the region is already inside, bindable to one bone, or unfixable by any weighting. Part/label-based ownership restriction (separate at every label boundary, weld the real joints back, then mask or decay the field by skeleton steps) is measured and recorded as a negative: it halves the head's lag but multiplies extreme joint tears. The rigid-part ("Toy Story") sweep behind that negative takes weights out of the argument - one bone per vertex, one bone per separated part - and attributes the faults: the head's lag is the field's blend (its own `Head` share 0.516 -> 1.000, slip 12.1% / 14.2% -> 0.00-0.16%, head edges back to exact rest length), while separating parts raises escaped vertices and tearing monotonically, so what remains is geometry. A weld is provable *bad*, never good: `owned_rerig/weld_topology.py` finds this unit is one welded piece with a single triangle fan at 3789 of 3796 nodes, so a fused prop leaves no trace and only the reference's plates can say it must come apart; the containment cut built on that reference evidence buys silhouette (escaped 830 -> 622 walk, 748 -> 516 shoot) at the cost of wrong anatomy and tripled tearing, so it is a lever rather than a repair. Part identification was then attacked directly: ownership from distance along the surface (585 of 3796 nodes relabelled) and a core-pinned harmonic diffusion were both measured and are both worse on escapes and cohesion, closing the geometry-only family; ownership from the reference's own motion (each bone's share multiplied by a per-node, per-bone plate-agreement factor - would this bone's own motion keep this flesh on the art?) is the first rule that cuts escapes and excess together (830 -> 378, 4.36% -> 1.79% at 1 px), and its remaining tear and seam costs are tabulated in the doc rather than settled. Gates: `tools/validate_homm3_reference_frame_bind_contract.py` (source contract, wired into the OfflineUnlockMod build), `tools/homm3_meshy_frame_pose/test_exact_reference_bind.py` and `tools/homm3_meshy_frame_pose/test_weld_topology.py`. Generated-artifact only, one unit and one clip; the idle and shoot clips are refused because the reference itself puts the hand/forearm off its plate.

## Active Runtime And Build Boundary

The active modded target is `V:\SteamLibrary\steamapps\common\Heroes of Might and Magic Olden Era - Golden Era`. `official_deobf`, `OfficialDeobfBuild`, and `OFFICIAL_DEOBF_BUILD` are compatibility/build identifiers, not install-selection evidence. Golden Era compile claims require the established `OfficialDeobfBuild=true` path and the configured live interop, not a plain ad hoc build.

Scenario and campaign readiness is defined by the current installer configuration and validators, not an old map SID named in a plan. Campaign maps belong in `StreamingAssets/maps/Story_maps/`; a second copy under `maps/` is a rejected dual registration.
